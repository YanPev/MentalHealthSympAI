Clinical NLP · Research results

Expanded retrieval & LLM evidence-judging for PHQ-8 severity prediction

An eight-phase study on whether a richer symptom dictionary and a real LLM evidence-quality judge improve depression-symptom scoring from clinical interviews — and an honest account of where they do and don't.

  • Data DAIC-WOZ · 219 participants · 1,752 item-rows
  • Task PHQ-8 · 8 items × 0–3
  • CV StratifiedGroupKFold(5, seed 42)
  • Compute BGU SLURM · rtx_3090 · ~12 GPU jobs
  • Branch models/bert-classifier

The result in one screen

Retrieval expansion helps the encoder, not the headline cascade

The expanded dictionary (R1) lifts the standalone encoder — especially its ability to catch severe symptoms — but does not beat the project-best merged cascade. The single largest metric gain came from elsewhere: an evidence-driven training policy that drops symptom items with no interview evidence.

What this means

Expanded retrieval is an encoder-level and severe-class-recovery win and an interpretability win — not a new state-of-the-art on the headline cascade metric. Bootstrap CIs on the encoder gains straddle zero, so they are suggestive, not significant. Reported plainly rather than oversold.

Phase 01

A tiered symptom lexicon

The original retrieval query for each PHQ-8 item was a hand-curated glossary of lay phrasings. Phase 1 wraps that frozen glossary as a core tier and layers two more: a synonyms tier of lay paraphrases and multi-word expressions for how participants actually talk, and a clinical tier in DSM-5 register (anhedonia, hypersomnia, psychomotor retardation, and so on). A one-off Qwen2.5-7B brainstorm proposed candidate terms offline; every term was reviewed by hand and merged with provenance comments before anything downstream could read it.

Phase 03 · the pivotal idea

A real evidence judge, and why keyword hit-rate lied

The motivating observation for the whole study: a keyword hit-rate massively over-counts evidence quality. When a Qwen judge is asked whether retrieved windows actually support a symptom, the picture collapses — most starkly for NoInterest, where lexical terms appear in 99.5% of windows but the judge confirms genuine support in only 2.3%.

Phase 04 · infrastructure & guards

Treating "no evidence" as missing, and proving nothing drifted

If the interview never touched a symptom, scoring it 0 is a guess, not a measurement. Phase 4 adds three training policies, applied after the fold split to the training portion only, so folds and out-of-fold coverage stay identical across policies:

  • zero — status quo; every row keeps its gold label.
  • mask — no-evidence rows stay in the batch but contribute no gradient (loss-masked; numerically identical to per-row zero-weighting).
  • drop — no-evidence rows are removed from training and from class-weight computation.

Two guards ran before any GPU spend. Fold-identity was proven exhaustively — all three expanded variants reproduce the frozen baseline's fold column row-for-row (1,752 / 1,752), so any measured delta is retrieval, not a reshuffle. And an eval-drift guard confirmed the metric code reproduces the frozen cascade's published numbers exactly, so later deltas are trustworthy. A dual-reporting metric (comprehensive_with_abstention) was also added for abstaining predictors: coverage, covered-subset quality, a 0-imputed headline total, and a prorated sensitivity total.

Phase 05

Retraining the stack on R1

Every model was retrained on the selected R1 retrieval, holding hyperparameters identical to the frozen baselines (MentalBERT + CORN, 8 epochs, seed 42; Attention-MIL; a staged-tolerant Qwen CoT pool at 5 self-consistency chains; and the mask/drop policy runs). The R1 CoT pool hit a GPU out-of-memory failure on four of five folds; an OOM fix and retry completed the pool cleanly (1,752 out-of-fold rows across the five folds). The cascade then recombines encoder confidence with the LLM using the frozen merged-gate parameters (τ = 0.8, diff ≥ 0.5) — reused, never re-tuned on the new data.

Phase 07 · clinical usability

Turning predictions into a clinician-facing report

Out-of-fold cascade predictions, the evidence judge's per-item status, and the questionnaire self-report are assembled into a per-participant schema, which Qwen renders into a fixed "Dear clinician…" narrative — the numbers come from the schema; the model only writes prose. Twelve example reports span the severity bands and the interesting disagreement cases. The design distinguishes five finding types, so what needs a clinician's attention reads at a glance:

Every report closes by stating it is model-generated decision support, not a diagnosis. (Individual reports contain interview excerpts and are kept out of this summary.)

Bottom line

An honest verdict

What worked

Expanding the retrieval dictionary consistently helped the standalone encoder, most visibly on the hardest and most clinically important slice — severe symptoms (class-3 F1 +0.064, severe recall +0.048). The 4-way LLM judge replaced a keyword proxy that over-counted evidence by up to 40×, and the evidence-driven drop training policy produced the single largest metric gain of the study (QWK .354 → .421).

What didn't — and the caveats

R1 did not beat the project-best merged cascade; the cascade regressed on QWK and MAE, the latter the only interval that clears zero. Every encoder gain is directionally positive but its bootstrap CI includes zero — suggestive, not significant. Severe-symptom under-calling (55% of gold-3 items predicted ≤ 1) remains the dominant open problem, especially where distress is expressed indirectly.

The most defensible framing: this work is an encoder-level and interpretability advance — a better evidence signal, an evidence-aware training regime, and clinician-facing outputs — rather than a new headline accuracy record. All selection was label-free or within training folds, folds are provably identical across every comparison, and the evaluation code reproduces the frozen baselines exactly.
