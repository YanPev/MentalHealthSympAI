Chain-of-Thought for PHQ-8 severity — executive summary

Can an LLM's chain-of-thought reasoning improve per-item depression-severity prediction over the tuned MentalBERT+CORN encoder? Twelve experiments, participant-grouped 5-fold out-of-fold (n=1752). This is the summary; the full step-by-step report is cot_full_report.html.

Headline result

A simple severity-routing ensemble of the encoder and a few-shot CoT LLM (Qwen-7B) is the best configuration found: macro-F1 0.377  ·  QWK 0.379
vs encoder alone 0.348 / 0.354  (Δ +0.029 macro-F1, +0.025 QWK) — and it beats the CoT alone too.

The two models are complementary: the LLM recovers the severe classes the encoder collapses on (class-3 F1 0.297 vs 0.159); the encoder keeps its errors ordinally closer. Routing — trust the LLM when it predicts a severe class (≥2), else the encoder — captures both strengths.

What worked, what didn't

WORKSGenuine value

  • The ensemble — severity-routing beats both parents on macro-F1 and QWK simultaneously. The core positive result.
  • Better evidence (W5 windows) — improved the ensemble's ordinal metrics; set the best config.
  • Item-adaptive evidence — choosing evidence per item (full transcript for evidence-starved items like Appetite/NoInterest, focused retrieval for well-covered ones) lifts standalone CoT QWK to 0.368 (from 0.306) with no macro-F1 loss. Principled, leakage-free — but doesn't beat the headline ensemble.

INSIGHTChanged our understanding

  • Full transcript halved the under-called-severe cases (215→105) — proving the severe misses were part retrieval, part label, not a pure label ceiling. But more context causes over-attribution, so the net is item-dependent.
  • Retrieval was an unnecessary crutch for the LLM — inherited from the encoder's 512-token limit; the full interview (~1.3k tokens) fits the LLM's context many times over.

NO GAINTested and bounded

  • Self-consistency — eroded the macro-F1 edge (voting regresses to the safe label).
  • Distillation to 1.5B — lost the severe-class recovery; the value is in the 7B's reasoning capacity, not transferable cheaply.
  • Domain model (MentaLLaMA 13B/7B) — older LLaMA-2 base reasons about severity worse than a modern 7B; over-predicts, poor format adherence.
  • Item-aware retrieval — helps weak BM25 (+0.034 macro-F1) but is subsumed by good semantic/hybrid retrieval.
  • Learned stacking — does not beat the hand-built routing rule.

Conclusions

  • CoT earns its place as an ensemble component, not a replacement. An untrained few-shot LLM genuinely complements a tuned encoder on this task.
  • Scaling/model levers don't help here — bigger context, more samples, distillation, and a domain model all failed. The useful signal is in the 7B's reasoning, and it's already captured.
  • The remaining ceiling is mostly in the labels. Three independent lines of evidence converge: error analysis (CoT reasoning is faithful; the gold label diverges from interview content), the retrieval diagnostic (some symptoms are barely verbalised), and the full-transcript test (only ~half the severe misses were recoverable with more context). PHQ-8 self-report is only loosely coupled to what people say in the interview — especially somatic items.

Recommended next steps

Ordered by expected value. The first is the only one likely to lift the headline ceiling; the rest are refinements or rigor.

  • 1. Get better labels (the real lever). The ceiling is a self-report-vs-content gap, so the highest-value move is data, not modelling: clinician-rated item severity, or annotations of symptoms actually expressed in the interview. Alternatively, reframe the target toward the clinical screening decision (PHQ-8 total ≥10) and evaluate there — the ensemble's severe-class recovery may matter most for sensitivity.
  • 2. Tame full-transcript over-attribution, then re-ensemble. Item-adaptive evidence already lifts standalone QWK; pairing it with a calibration/abstention prompt (let the LLM defer to the encoder when evidence is thin) could let it beat 0.377 in the ensemble rather than just match it.
  • 3. Item-aware expansion on the production hybrid retriever. Our test used a weaker MiniLM reimplementation; applying query expansion to the real off-branch hybrid is the one retrieval experiment that could still fairly beat the pipeline (most promising on Sleep/Failure/Appetite).
  • 4. Multi-seed confirmation. All headline numbers are single-seed; confirm the ensemble gain and the small item-adaptive QWK improvement across seeds before publishing.
  • 5. Per-item ensemble combiner. The routing rule is global; a per-item route/blend (informed by the item-adaptive selection) is a cheap, no-GPU refinement.
Honesty notes. Single seed; participant-grouped 5-fold OOF; DAIC-WOZ is public so the LLM may have seen related data; severe-class counts are small so per-class numbers are high-variance; the item-aware-hybrid test used a MiniLM reimplementation weaker than the production retriever; the domain model was judged on canary folds. All gains beyond the ensemble are modest and should be multi-seed-confirmed.
