Adding Chain-of-Thought reasoning to the PHQ-8 pipeline

A complete, step-by-step investigation: can an LLM's chain-of-thought reasoning improve per-item depression-severity prediction over the tuned MentalBERT+CORN encoder? Feasibility → validation → self-consistency → ensemble → distillation → better evidence → error analysis → stacking → retrieval diagnostics → item-aware retrieval → domain model → full transcript → item-adaptive evidence.
🤖 Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (teacher) · 1.5B (student)🆚 MentalBERT + CORN🖥️ RTX 3090 / SLURM (offline)📊 participant-grouped 5-fold OOF, n=1752

Executive summary

An off-the-shelf LLM doing few-shot chain-of-thought (no training) is genuinely complementary to the trained encoder: it recovers the severe classes the encoder collapses on, while the encoder keeps errors ordinally closer. Neither dominates alone — but a simple severity-routing ensemble beats both parents on the headline metrics. Self-consistency, distillation to a small model, richer evidence, and a learned stacker were all tried; only better evidence nudged the best config further. An error analysis then showed the residual errors are largely a self-report-vs-interview-content ceiling, not a modelling gap. A per-item retrieval diagnostic localised that ceiling (some symptoms are barely retrieved at all), and an item-aware retrieval lever was prototyped: it genuinely helps weak (BM25) retrieval (+0.034 macro-F1) but is largely subsumed by good semantic retrieval and did not beat the production hybrid pipeline.

Best result: severity-routed(encoder, 7B-CoT-W5) — macro-F1 0.377, QWK 0.379 vs encoder 0.348/0.354 and CoT-alone 0.373/0.312.

Setup

Task. Predict each of the 8 PHQ-8 item severities (0–3) for a participant from retrieved snippets of their clinical interview (DAIC-WOZ). Baseline. The project's best encoder — MentalBERT fine-tuned with the CORN ordinal loss on Hybrid-W3 retrieved context windows (pooled OOF macro-F1 0.348). CoT idea. Give a capable open LLM the same evidence and have it reason about the PHQ-8 frequency anchors (0 not-at-all … 3 nearly-every-day) before answering. Everything runs offline on the cluster's 3090s; the comparison is paired on identical rows/folds.

Step 1 · Feasibility & 5-fold validation

A few-shot CoT probe (4 crafted exemplars mapping evidence → anchor) was run on all 5 folds and scored on the identical rows as the encoder. Untrained CoT beats the trained encoder on macro-F1 — validated across folds, not a single-fold fluke.

Step 1 · per-fold macro-F1 — CoT vs encoder

Pooled: CoT macro-F1 0.373 ± per-fold spread vs encoder 0.348 (CoT wins 4/5 folds). The decisive detail is per-class:

Step 1 · per-class F1 (class 3 = most severe)

CoT recovers the most-severe class (class-3 F1 0.295 vs encoder 0.159). But it loses the ordinal metrics — QWK 0.312 vs 0.354, MAE 0.750 vs 0.680 — because when wrong it is wrong by more. The two models are complementary, agreeing on only 45% of items.
NO GAIN

Step 2 · Self-consistency

CoT's weakness was far-off errors, so we sampled 5 reasoning chains and majority-voted (which also yields calibrated probabilities). It mechanically cut far-off errors and improved MAE a little — but eroded the macro-F1 edge (voting regresses toward the safe/modal label, killing the bold severe-class calls).

Step 2 · self-consistency (×5) vs greedy CoT

Self-consistency is not the lever: macro-F1 0.373→0.363, and it still trails the encoder on QWK/MAE. The ordinal gap is structural, not variance. Greedy decoding stays the better CoT config.
WIN

Step 3 · The ensemble — capturing both strengths

Given the complementarity, we combined the two models (no extra training). A prior-motivated severity-routing rule — trust the CoT label when it predicts a severe class (≥2), else the encoder — beats both parents on the headline metrics simultaneously.

Step 4 · Distillation to a cheap small model

The only thing holding the CoT component back is that it is a 7B model. We distilled "step-by-step": the 7B teacher wrote 1752 label-conditioned rationales, then a 1.5B student was LoRA-fine-tuned per fold (leakage-safe) to reproduce reasoning+answer.

Step 4 · distilled 1.5B student vs its 7B teacher

The 1.5B lost the severe-class recovery (class-3 F1 0.295→0.155) that was the 7B's entire value, scoring even below the encoder on macro-F1 (0.323). Routing to its now-unreliable severe calls actively hurts. The value lives in the 7B's reasoning capacity; shrinking it naively throws it away.
SMALL GAIN

Step 5 · Better evidence (Hybrid-W5 + per-excerpt formatting)

The CoT had been reasoning over a shuffled keyword blob. We fed it wider W5 windows rendered as separate coherent excerpts. This did not raise CoT's standalone macro-F1 — but it improved the best ensemble on its weak axis.

Step 5 · CoT with W5 evidence vs W3 evidence

Step 5 · best ensemble: W5 vs W3 evidence (macro-F1)

New best: severity-routed(encoder, W5-CoT) — macro-F1 0.377, QWK 0.379 (the best ordinal agreement of any CoT method; MAE also improved). Better evidence helps the combination's ordinal metrics, not CoT's standalone macro-F1 — a hint we were nearing a ceiling.
CEILING

Step 6 · Error analysis & learned stacking

We read the 317 far-off (≥2) cases together with their evidence and rationale. 215 of 317 are under-predictions (model says 0/1, self-report says 2/3), concentrated on somatic/internal items.

Step 6 · far-off errors by item (total 317)
somatic/internal items dominate

In both directions the CoT reasoning is faithful to the evidence — it is the gold label that diverges from what the interview content supports:

Under-call (Appetite, gold=2→0): evidence is all grief and hopelessness, no mention of eating. The model correctly notes there is no appetite signal.
Under-call (Sleep, gold=2→0): transcript says "most of the time it's pretty easy" to sleep — directly contradicting the self-reported score.
Over-call (Tired, gold=1→3): "always tired and lethargic, not excited about things" — the interview sounds severe, but self-report is mild.
This is a self-report ↔ interview-content ceiling. PHQ-8 self-report is only loosely coupled to what people verbalize (especially somatic items), so no amount of prompt/evidence engineering on the transcript can recover a label the transcript does not express. Consistent with the known top-2 (0.72) vs top-1 (0.43) accuracy gap. Only ~25% of misses are fixable (temporal reasoning: counting past/resolved symptoms as current) — and since under- and over-calls are opposite errors, nudging one worsens the other.

Learned stacking (last attempt at a free gain)

A nested-CV meta-classifier over the encoder + CoT probabilities can sit at either end of the F1↔MAE trade-off but does not beat the simple hand rule.

Step 6 · learned stacker vs the hand routing rule (macro-F1)

Learned stacking ≈ but does not dominate severity-routing. The hand rule, motivated directly by the error structure, remains best.
DIAGNOSE

Step 7 · Retrieval diagnostic — is the evidence even there?

The error analysis pointed upstream: maybe retrieval doesn't surface the symptom for some items. We measured, per item, how often the retrieved windows contain a symptom-relevant term.

Step 7 · keyword-hit rate per item (does retrieval surface the symptom?)
Fraction of examples whose hybrid windows contain a symptom-relevant term. Sleep 0.90 vs Moving 0.35 — some items rarely retrieve the symptom at all.

Relevance varies sharply: Sleep surfaces a relevant term 90% of the time, Moving only 35%, and Appetite has the weakest retrieval scores. The link to errors is real but confounded by label skew (Moving is ~77% label-0, so easy despite poor retrieval; overall keyword-hit↔far-off r=−0.13). Still, this says part of the difficulty is upstream of reasoning — and suggests one untried lever: query with each symptom's own vocabulary.
WIN

Step 8 · Item-aware retrieval (BM25) — the lever works

We expand each item's retrieval query with the symptom's vocabulary (synonyms, lay phrasings) and re-retrieve. Tested cleanly downstream: same window bank, same k, only the query differs — so the delta isolates query expansion.

Step 8 · item-aware vs bare query — BM25 retrieval (same bank, same k)

Step 8 · per-item macro-F1 (item-aware vs bare BM25 query)

Query expansion genuinely helps weak retrieval: macro-F1 0.294→0.328 (+0.033), QWK +0.060, with the per-item gains landing where retrieval relevance improved (Sleep, Failure, Appetite). Caveat: this is BM25; both conditions still trail the production hybrid retrieval (0.360) — so the question is whether the lever survives on the stronger retriever.
NO GAIN

Step 9 · Item-aware HYBRID retrieval — doesn't beat production

We re-implemented hybrid retrieval (MiniLM semantic + BM25 fusion) and ran it with the bare vs item-aware query, then compared to the production W5-hybrid CoT.

Step 9 · item-aware vs bare query — within hybrid retrieval

Step 9 · both my hybrid conditions vs the production W5-hybrid CoT (macro-F1)

Within hybrid, item-aware expansion gives no macro-F1 gain (0.326→0.325) — the semantic component already captures topical relevance, so keyword expansion is largely redundant (it still lifts QWK and severe-class F1 but worsens MAE, with large per-item swings). And both my hybrid conditions trail the production W5-hybrid (0.360/0.306). Conclusion: the item-aware lever's value is real for weak retrieval but is subsumed by good semantic retrieval — it does not beat the current pipeline. The standing best is unchanged.
NO GAIN

Step 10 · Domain model (MentaLLaMA) — underperforms

We swapped the LLM for the mental-health-tuned MentaLLaMA (LLaMA-2), same W5 evidence/folds. Both sizes lost to Qwen-7B on a full canary fold (n=352): 13B macro-F1 0.285 / QWK 0.239 (over-predicts severity, 12% unparseable); 7B macro-F1 0.227 / QWK 0.114 (37% unparseable). Qwen-7B fold-1 = 0.325 / 0.297.

Domain tuning didn't help: the older LLaMA-2 base reasons about PHQ-8 severity worse than a modern general 7B, and follows the structured-output format far less reliably. Not fanned out beyond the canaries. Qwen-7B remains the CoT.
INSIGHT

Step 11 · Full transcript — partly overturns the ceiling

Realisation: full DAIC transcripts are only ~1.3k tokens (max 4.5k), but the CoT had been fed just ~350 tokens of retrieved windows — a retrieval crutch inherited from the encoder's 512-token limit, irrelevant to a 32k-context LLM. So we fed Qwen-7B the whole interview per item.

Step 11 · full transcript vs focused W5 retrieval (Qwen-7B)

Under-called severe cases dropped 215→105 (roughly halved) — proving those misses were ~half a retrieval miss, not a pure label ceiling: full context recovers symptoms retrieval hid.

Step 11 · per-item macro-F1 (full transcript vs W5) — gains on starved items

But it's not a free win — total far-off rose and MAE worsened (0.735→0.919): full context causes over-attribution (the model finds "evidence" everywhere and over-calls). Net macro-F1 drops (0.360→0.330). The per-item pattern is the real signal — gains on the evidence-starved items (Appetite, NoInterest) and losses on well-retrieved ones (Depressed, Sleep). The optimal amount of evidence is item-dependent.
WIN

Step 12 · Item-adaptive evidence — a principled improvement

Acting on Step 11: per item, pick the better evidence source (full transcript vs focused W5) using only the other folds, applied to the held-out fold (leakage-free). The selection is stable — Appetite/NoInterest→full (5/5 folds), Depressed/Sleep/Moving→W5 (5/5).

Step 12 · standalone CoT ordinal agreement (QWK) by evidence strategy

Step 12 · item-adaptive vs focused-W5 evidence (standalone CoT)

Item-adaptive evidence holds W5's macro-F1 (0.360) while lifting standalone CoT QWK to 0.368 (from 0.306) — the best ordinal agreement of any standalone CoT, and a clean confirmation that evidence should be chosen per item.
It does not beat the headline best, though: the severity-routing ensemble with item-adaptive lands at 0.355 macro-F1 (routing to the over-predicting full-transcript items hurts precision), below severity-routed(W5)'s 0.377. Its nested-CV blend reaches MAE 0.675 (best anywhere) — a better-balanced operating point — but the macro-F1 champion is unchanged.

Conclusion & recommendation

The investigation establishes a clean, defensible result: an LLM chain-of-thought component genuinely complements the encoder, and a severity-routing ensemble of the two is the best configuration found — macro-F1 0.377, QWK 0.379, beating both the tuned encoder and the CoT alone. Across twelve steps every other lever was tested and bounded: self-consistency, naive distillation, learned stacking, a domain-tuned model, and item-aware retrieval (BM25 & hybrid) added no headline gain. Two levers gave real but non-dominant value — better evidence (W5) improved the ensemble's ordinals, and item-adaptive evidence (Step 12), motivated by the full-transcript finding, lifted standalone CoT QWK to 0.368 while holding macro-F1. The macro-F1 champion is unchanged throughout.

The frontier is largely reached — with one nuance. The full-transcript experiment (Step 11) showed the severe-class misses were part retrieval, part label: feeding the whole interview halved the under-called-severe count (215→105), so retrieval — not just labels — was capping the model. But more context over-attributes elsewhere, so the net is a wash and the gains are item-dependent. The deeper ceiling remains a label/self-report gap (the error analysis: CoT reasoning is faithful, the gold label diverges from interview content; the retrieval diagnostic: some symptoms are barely verbalised). Further headline gains likely need different labels/data — clinician severity ratings, or an observable-behaviour target — not more LLM/retrieval engineering. Honesty notes: single seed; participant-grouped 5-fold OOF; DAIC-WOZ is public so the LLM may have seen related data; severe-class counts are small so per-class numbers are high-variance; Step 9's hybrid is a MiniLM reimplementation weaker than the production retriever; MentaLLaMA (Step 10) was judged on canary folds only.
